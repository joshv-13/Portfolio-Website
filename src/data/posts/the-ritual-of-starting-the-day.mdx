A good morning doesn’t happen by accident. It’s **designed**—usually with a few small actions you repeat until they become automatic.

## The goal

The goal isn’t “perfect mornings.” It’s to start your day with:

- momentum
- clarity
- a small win
- fewer decisions

## My 10-minute baseline ritual

When I’m busy, I keep it brutally simple:

1. **Water** (before anything else)
2. **Light movement** (pushups, a short walk, mobility)
3. **Two minutes of stillness** (breathing, prayer, or silence)
4. **Write one line**: *What would make today a win?*

That’s it.

## Why it works

A morning ritual works because it reduces cognitive load. You stop negotiating with yourself.

Instead of asking *“What should I do first?”* you just start.

## A simple upgrade: the “one hard thing”

If I’m feeling stuck, I add one rule:

> Do one hard thing before noon.

It can be a difficult email, a workout, or 25 minutes on your most important project.

## Closing thought

You don’t need a long ritual. You need a **repeatable** one.

Start small. Make it easy to win. Let it compound.
