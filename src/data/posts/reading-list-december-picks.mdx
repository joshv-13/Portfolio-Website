I don’t read to collect facts.
I read to **change how I think or act**.

Here are the books that stood out this month — and why.

## 1. *The Art of Learning* — Josh Waitzkin

A book about learning that’s actually about **pressure**.

**Key takeaway:**  
Skill is built by deliberately training your weakest positions, not your strengths.

**Action:**  
Identify one area where you feel uncomfortable and practice it in isolation.

## 2. *Four Thousand Weeks* — Oliver Burkeman

A sober reminder that time management is mostly self-deception.

**Key takeaway:**  
You don’t need better systems — you need fewer commitments.

**Action:**  
Remove one recurring obligation that no longer matters.

## 3. *Poor Charlie’s Almanack* — Charlie Munger

Dense, slow, and worth it.

**Key takeaway:**  
Most bad decisions come from using one mental model instead of many.

**Action:**  
When stuck, ask: *What would this look like through a different discipline?*

## How I choose what to read

My filter is simple:

- Does it change behavior?
- Does it survive a reread?
- Does it connect ideas across domains?

If not, I move on quickly.

## Closing thought

Reading is leverage — but only if you apply it.

One good idea, used consistently, beats ten that stay on the shelf.
