Most difficult decisions aren’t hard because we lack information.
They’re hard because we’re trying to **optimize for everything at once**.

Over time, I’ve settled on a framework that removes noise and forces clarity.

## Step 1: Define the decision boundary

Write the decision as a sentence:

> *“I am deciding whether to X or Y.”*

If you can’t do this clearly, you’re not ready to decide.

## Step 2: Identify irreversible vs reversible

Ask one question:

> *Can this be undone at low cost?*

- If **yes**, bias toward action.
- If **no**, slow down and reduce downside.

Most decisions are more reversible than they feel.

## Step 3: Optimize for regret, not outcome

Instead of asking *“What’s the best result?”* ask:

> *Which option will I regret not trying?*

Regret is easier to forecast than success.

## Step 4: Eliminate false tradeoffs

Many decisions feel binary when they’re not.

Ask:
- Can I run a small experiment?
- Can I delay part of the commitment?
- Can I do both, temporarily?

Constraints create creativity.

## Step 5: Decide once

Once you decide:
- Write down **why**
- Commit fully
- Stop reopening the loop

Decision fatigue comes from second-guessing, not from deciding.

## Closing thought

A good framework won’t guarantee perfect outcomes.
It will guarantee **forward motion** — and that’s usually enough.
