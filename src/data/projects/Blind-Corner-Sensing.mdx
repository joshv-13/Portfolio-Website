## Overview

Blind intersections are a difficult place for people and mobile robots to share space. Walls, shelving, and machinery can hide approaching traffic until it reaches the crossing. This project investigates how a compact infrastructure-mounted sensor could provide an additional view of those approaches.

Working with the University of Waterloo research team, I investigated a **single 60 GHz FMCW radar paired with a fixed printed reflectarray**, described in the paper as a fixed RIS. The module retains useful forward sensing while redirecting energy toward a selected rear-side region, providing complementary coverage with one active RF sensor.

Experiments demonstrated human and robot detection, range tracking, apparent angle-of-arrival estimation, and motion classification. The prototype is a sensing foundation for future collision-awareness systems; robot communication, worker warnings, and automatic stopping were not implemented.

- A compact **Infineon BGT60TR13C radar–RIS module** with a custom 3D-printed fixture

## Complementary Coverage at a Blind Corner

The system uses two propagation paths:

- **Direct path:** radar → target → radar
- **Redirected path:** radar → reflectarray → target → reflectarray → radar

The printed phase pattern compensates for the curved illumination from the nearby radar feed. The approximately **40 mm aperture** operates with a fixed map, illuminating a selected direction rather than electronically scanning the entire secondary region.

## Experimental Setup & Data Collection

The radar uses **one transmit channel and three receive channels**, sweeping approximately **58.25–63.25 GHz**. Its roughly **5 GHz bandwidth** gives a nominal **3 cm range resolution**. Each frame contains **64 chirps**, with **134 samples per chirp**, at approximately **12.94 frames per second**.

The evaluation progressed through three stages:

1. **System characterization:** approach–retreat measurements to examine usable range and angular response, plus matched hardware comparisons.
2. **Application sensing:** repeated entry/exit trials and extended human and robot motion recordings for tracking and classification.
3. **Blind-corner trials:** human and robot approaches from the forward and rear regions at an indoor intersection, with three repetitions of each condition for **12 trials in total**.

Application measurements used **one human participant and one robot**, so the results characterize the tested setup rather than generalization across workers, robot platforms, or factories.

## Signal Processing Pipeline

The application pipeline extracts both position-related information and motion structure:

1. Remove the fast-time mean, apply a Hann window, and compute the **range FFT**.
2. Detect candidate targets using range-dependent background and local-contrast checks, with continuity filtering for the selected range track.
3. Extract complex samples around the detected target range.
4. Apply chirp-to-chirp **moving-target indication (MTI)** and a Doppler FFT to obtain target-gated micro-Doppler profiles.
5. Estimate the dominant Doppler centroid, align the profiles to it, and normalize their power.
6. Classify temporal variation in the aligned profiles and calculate apparent azimuth from receiver phase relationships.

Centroid alignment reduces the influence of bulk translation, helping expose differences between articulated walking motion and predominantly rigid robot motion. For the reflected path, the measured angle is an **apparent arrival angle**, not a direct measurement of the target's physical bearing behind the module.

## Human–Robot Motion Classification

Human walking produces a broader, time-varying Doppler structure as different body parts move at different velocities. The robot example has a narrower, more stable ridge around its motion centroid.

![Centroid-aligned micro-Doppler examples for human and robot movement](/Portfolio-Website/images/projects/blind-corner-sensing/micro-doppler.png)
*Human motion on the left and robot motion on the right. These normalized examples emphasize relative spectral structure, not absolute received-power differences.*

The classifier uses **temporal variance across relative-velocity bins** over **16-frame windows**, advanced by four frames. Standardized features feed an **RBF support vector machine**. Grouped cross-validation keeps all windows from a recording in the same fold.

| True class | Evaluated windows | Correct predictions | Recall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Human | 642 | 642 | 100.0% |
| Robot | 778 | 654 | 84.1% |

The displayed evaluation contains **1,296 correct predictions out of 1,420 windows**, giving approximately **91.3% accuracy**. All 124 errors are robot windows classified as human. These are window-level results from the recorded subjects, not independent-person or unseen-robot validation.

## Results: Range, Tracking & Blind-Corner Trials

- **Human detection:** approximately **5 m** along the RIS-assisted path; the illustrated angular experiment reaches **4.69 m at 40°**.
- **Robot detection:** approximately **6 m** along the RIS-assisted path, as reported in the paper.
- **Forward sensing retained:** robot range tracks and changing apparent azimuth remain observable with the reflectarray installed.
- **Combined outputs:** four representative intersection trials show accepted range tracks, apparent angles, and human–robot predictions together.

## Hardware Comparison

The paper reports the following target-return contrasts using a **shared full-recording RIS reference**:

| Configuration | Rear-section contrast | Forward-section contrast |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Radar + fixed RIS | **10.99 dB** | 13.97 dB |
| Radar only | 7.50 dB | **20.49 dB** |
| Radar + metal sheet | 5.97 dB | 6.18 dB |

In the rear straight section, the RIS configuration exceeds radar only by **3.49 dB** and the metal sheet by **5.02 dB**. Radar only produces the strongest forward-section ratio, while the RIS configuration has the lowest estimated background in both sections.

This is a coverage trade-off: the surface improves the reported rear-section response while preserving useful forward sensing. The contrasts describe the complete tested configurations and their processing references; they are **not calibrated SNR or sensitivity gains**, nor an isolated measurement of the binary phase pattern's contribution.

## Key Learnings & Next Steps

- **A passive aperture can extend the useful coverage of one radar.** Its benefit depends on the intersection geometry and selected sensing direction.
- **Motion structure helps distinguish people and robots.** Centroid-aligned micro-Doppler features capture information beyond target range and bulk speed.
- **Region assignment remains a central challenge.** Multiple returns can be observed, but reliable branch assignment and simultaneous multi-target tracking require further development.
- **Electronic reconfiguration could expand coverage.** Selectable phase maps could address different rear directions while preserving the forward view.
- **Deployment needs broader validation and controller integration.** Next steps include additional people and robots, independent trajectory ground truth, response-time measurements, and defined behavior when detections are uncertain.
