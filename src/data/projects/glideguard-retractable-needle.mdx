## Overview

Needlestick injuries are a persistent safety risk for healthcare workers, especially during recapping and disposal. **GlideGuard** is a needle-tip attachment that **automatically covers the needle after use** using a spring-driven sliding sheath and latch mechanism—reducing exposure without requiring a workflow change or a full retractable syringe.

The key design choice was to **use a sliding sheath instead of retracting the needle into the syringe barrel**, preserving compatibility with standard syringes and reducing inventory complexity.

---

## Problem

Healthcare workers administer injections at massive scale, and even low-probability errors become frequent events. Most injuries occur during **handling after injection**—particularly recapping and disposal—when fatigue and time pressure are highest.

Existing solutions often trade safety for cost or usability:
- Full retractable syringes are effective, but increase inventory burden and reduce interchangeability.
- Some retractable needle tips require hand placement near the needle or introduce awkward activation steps.

GlideGuard was designed to prioritize:
- **Safety (minimize needle exposure)**
- **Ease of implementation (minimal workflow change)**
- **Manufacturing simplicity and cost competitiveness**

---

## Solution Summary

GlideGuard is a **spring-loaded sheath** that:
- Ships with the needle covered (safe out of the package)
- Latches in a retracted position for injection
- Releases automatically after injection to re-cover the needle

### What makes it different
- Keeps the needle + syringe **interchangeable** via **standard Luer lock**
- Uses **mechanical auto-actuation** during the injection/removal motion
- Minimizes moving parts while maintaining robust locking

## How It Works

![Three-Stage Use Diagram](/Portfolio-Website/images/projects/glideguard/three-stage.png)

### 1) Initial State (Shipped Safe)
The sheath starts **covering the needle tip**, preventing accidental sticks pre-use.

### 2) Injection State (Latched Open)
The healthcare worker pulls the sheath back to expose the needle and **engage the latch**.

### 3) Release State (Auto-Sheathed)
During injection, the sheath compresses against the skin and disengages the latch.
When the needle is withdrawn, the spring drives the sheath forward, **covering the needle tip immediately**.

## Mechanical Architecture

![Exploded View](/Portfolio-Website/images/projects/glideguard/exploded.png)

GlideGuard was designed to be simple and scalable:

- **Needle hub**
  - Interfaces with standard syringes through Luer lock
  - Includes latch geometry for sheath engagement
- **Sheath**
  - Transparent polymer body (maintains visibility of syringe markings)
  - Interior tabs interface with hub latch
  - Rounded/concave end geometry to avoid pinch points
- **Compression spring**
  - Drives forward motion after latch release
- **Needle tubing**
  - Purchased in bulk and cut to length as required

## Latch Mechanism

![Latch Mechanism Steps](/Portfolio-Website/images/projects/glideguard/latch-steps.png)

The latch uses a repeatable axial-load interaction (conceptually similar to a pen-style latch), designed to:
- Hold the sheath retracted during injection
- Release reliably when conditions are met
- Avoid uneven loading via a mirrored latch feature

Primary design goals:
- **Minimize failure points**
- **Reduce risk of pinch points**
- **Prevent accidental post-use exposure**

## Design Constraints and Standards

The design was developed with medical device constraints in mind, including:
- Compatibility with standard needle gauges and syringe systems
- Sterile, tamper-proof packaging and bulk distribution requirements
- Sharps-injury prevention expectations and usability constraints
- Manufacturability at high volume (injection molding + simple assembly)

---

## Engineering Validation

To support feasibility, the project included quantitative checks such as:
- Spring force selection (enough to overcome friction and reliably sheath)
- Energy stored in spring compression during use
- Stress estimates to ensure components remain within safe mechanical limits

These calculations informed:
- Material choice for the sheath and hub
- Spring constant targets
- Geometry decisions for tab + latch reliability

---

## Economics and Scalability

A core goal was to remain cost-competitive while improving safety.

GlideGuard was designed to:
- Be manufacturable using injection molding
- Require few components
- Avoid replacing the entire syringe system (unlike full retractable syringes)

The analysis indicated that even modest reductions in needlestick incidence could create meaningful net savings at healthcare-system scale, while keeping per-unit cost competitive at volume.

## Risks and Next Steps

Key uncertainties identified for future development:
- Extensive reliability testing across environmental conditions (humidity, tolerance stacking)

- User trials to confirm it integrates seamlessly with real workflows
