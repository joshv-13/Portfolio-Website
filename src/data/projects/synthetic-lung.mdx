## Overview

The **Human Respiration Simulator** is a mechanically actuated, anatomically scaled model designed to demonstrate the true physics of breathing.

Most anatomy tools show structure but not function.  
This system was engineered to physically demonstrate that respiration is driven by **pressure gradients within a sealed thoracic cavity**, not by the lungs actively inflating themselves.

The device replicates:

- Negative intrapleural pressure mechanics  
- Independent left and right lung compartments  
- Reversible unilateral lung collapse (pneumothorax)  
- Anatomically realistic spatial constraints  

The result is a functional synthetic lung system that makes respiratory mechanics visually and mechanically intuitive.

## Breathing Mechanics

![Breathing Mechanics Diagram](/images/projects/synthetic-lung/breathing-mechanics.jpg)

Breathing is governed by changes in thoracic volume.

**Inhalation**
- Diaphragm contracts downward  
- Thoracic cavity volume increases  
- Intrapleural pressure decreases  
- Lungs expand passively  
- Air flows inward  

**Exhalation**
- Diaphragm relaxes  
- Thoracic volume decreases  
- Pressure increases  
- Air is expelled  

This pressure–volume relationship forms the mechanical foundation of the simulator.

## System Architecture

### Structural Frame
- Anatomically proportioned thoracic skeleton  
- Defines rib cage geometry and mediastinal boundaries  

### Dual Pleural Compartments
- Independent silicone-lined chambers  
- Airtight isolation between left and right cavities  
- Mimic parietal and visceral pleura  

### Synthetic Lungs
- Latex bladder balloons  
- Expand passively in response to negative pressure  

### Diaphragm Assembly
- Elastic neoprene membrane  
- Manual downward actuation  
- Generates controllable negative intrathoracic pressure  

### Pneumothorax Simulation Valve
- Rib-mounted air valve  
- Allows atmospheric air into one pleural compartment  
- Eliminates pressure gradient  
- Causes unilateral lung collapse  
- Fully reversible

## Engineering Decisions

A hybrid manual–pneumatic architecture was selected to balance:

- Mechanical realism  
- Cost efficiency  
- Operational simplicity  
- Classroom safety  

The design prioritizes:

- Clear visualization over hidden complexity  
- Anatomical proportion over cosmetic detail  
- Mechanical robustness over automation  

---

## Constraints

The system was developed under:

- Strict prototype budget limitations  
- Short development timeline  
- Anatomically realistic torso-scale requirement  
- Safe classroom operation standards  

All structural edges were enclosed, and pressure gradients were maintained within safe limits.

---

## Limitations

- No active rib cage expansion (intercostal motion not actuated)  
- Manual diaphragm actuation  
- Single pathology module (pneumothorax)  
- No integrated pressure sensing  

Future iterations may incorporate motorized actuation and quantitative pressure feedback.

## Key Outcomes

This project demonstrates:

- Translation of physiological principles into mechanical systems  
- Pressure-gradient modeling in a physical device  
- Compartment isolation architecture  
- Cost-constrained biomedical prototyping  
- Educational device engineering  

It reinforces a fundamental concept:

> Respiration is a pressure-driven mechanical process governed by thoracic volume changes.
